The stage is a layer

Home is six horizontal bands between the status strip and the navigation, and one band is one widget row. The stage claims the top n of them and keeps running — navigation switches only the region below it, and tapping a widget expands it in place down there rather than taking over the screen. As cards get built, they become destinations in that lower region.

WHAT IS SETTLED AND WHAT IS NOT

CLIMATE AND STEREO ARE PARKED — AND ONE CONFLICTS WITH C3

Both are nav slots here, not designed cards. Worth knowing before they get built: constraint C3 and ADR-0006 both say plainly — no takeover of audio, factory camera, or climate.

A Stereo card is fine as long as it drives DashDeck's own playback on the tablet rather than remote-controlling SYNC 3; that is additive. A Climate card that actually drives the factory HVAC is not — it needs an ADR superseding ADR-0006, means writing to an undocumented Ford network, and fails several of the five gates as written. Logged as Q14 so the reasoning survives outside this conversation.

SETTLED THIS ROUND

  • The stage is a layer, not a screen. It keeps running while navigation switches the region below it — maps does not stop because you opened a card. That gives the stage its own lifecycle in the component contract.
  • Widgets expand in place. Tapping one opens history and detail below the stage; the stage yields bands instead of being replaced. No separate full-screen component view, so the old Trip Computer screen is gone.
  • Six bands are a shared budget. Whatever is below asks for what it needs and the stage takes the remainder, down to one band. Settings is the exception that takes all six.
  • Nav is the destination list for the lower region and grows with the cards. One band = 195px (175 widget + 20 gutter), so six is exactly 1170 and the whole screen is exactly 1440.

STILL OPEN

  • Nav overflow. Four destinations fit comfortably at 240 wide. Past about five the strip has to scroll or paginate, and I have not designed that — it matters as soon as cards start landing.
  • What fills the stage first — video, maps or media. Maps is drawn throughout, but nothing behind it exists. Android Auto still cannot fill it (Q13): it is phone projection with no Windows client.
  • Video while moving. I drew a Parked chip and assumed video is stationary-only. My call, not yours, and easy to remove.
  • Live gauges (Q12). The speed widget updates continuously, which the unmeasured throughput ceiling has not earned yet.
